Make a LoRA

A LoRA is a small add-on file that teaches FLUX.2 klein one thing it does not know: a style, a character, an object. You make it from your own pictures, with mflux's trainer, on this Mac. HikariLM does not train; this page walks you through doing it in Terminal. HikariLM draws with LoRAs on FLUX.2 klein base 4B.

Before you start. Set up making pictures first (the picture setup page), so mflux is installed in ~/.hikarilm/pictures-venv. Training takes hours and uses the whole Mac, so stop HikariLM while it runs (hikarilm stop) and start it again after (hikarilm start).

1 Download the base 4B

LoRAs are trained on the base model, not the quick 4-step one. mflux-community's 4-bit copy (4.6 GB):

~/.hikarilm/venv/bin/hf download mflux-community/flux-2-klein-base-4b-mflux-q4

The 8-bit copy, mflux-community/flux-2-klein-base-4b-mflux-q8 (8.6 GB), keeps more detail. If you take it, change model_path in step 4 to match.

2 Gather the pictures

Put them in one folder, each with a caption file of the same name:

dataset/
  001.png
  001.txt
  002.png
  002.txt
  …
  preview_1.txt
  • PNG, JPEG or WebP. Any size; the long side is brought down to 1024.
  • Every picture needs its .txt; the trainer stops and names the one that has none.
  • Variety teaches more than numbers: the thing you want, in different poses, places, lights and framings. Leave out blurry pictures and near-copies.
  • preview_1.txt, preview_2.txt… (optional) are prompts it draws every so often while it trains, so you can see the LoRA take hold. Write them the way you will prompt later, with the trigger word (step 3), such as zq_usagi, riding a bicycle by the sea. Pick something that is in none of your pictures, so you can see whether it learned the character or just copies the pictures.

3 Write the captions

The captions decide what the LoRA learns, more than anything else on this page.

The trigger word

A trigger word is a name you make up for the thing the LoRA learns. It goes at the start of every caption, and later in every prompt where you want the LoRA to show. While training, the model sees the word next to your pictures again and again, and comes to read it as "this character" or "this style".

It must be a word the model has never seen, such as zq_usagi or zq_style. A real word already means something: train on rabbit and you blur your character with every rabbit the model knows, and every rabbit you draw afterwards turns into yours. Keep it short, with no spaces, and the same in every caption.

Describe what changes, leave out what the LoRA should learn

For every picture, the model tries to explain what it sees with the caption. What the caption names, it puts down to those words. What the caption never names, but is in every picture, it can only put down to the one word that is in every caption: the trigger word. So:

  • Describe what is different in each picture: the pose, the place, the clothes that change, the light, the framing. Those stay yours to choose in a prompt.
  • Leave out what is the same in every picture and is what you want: a character's face, fur and colours, or a style's brushwork and palette. That goes into the trigger word.

Example: a character. Thirty pictures of Usagi, a white rabbit girl with long ears tipped pink, red eyes and a blue scarf. Trigger word zq_usagi.

The pictureCaption
Usagi on a park bench in the rain, with an umbrellazq_usagi, sitting on a park bench, holding a red umbrella, rainy evening, side view
Usagi waving in a kitchenzq_usagi, standing in a kitchen, waving, smiling, morning light, close-up
Usagi asleep under a treezq_usagi, asleep under a cherry tree, petals falling, wide shot

None of them says white fur, pink-tipped ears, red eyes or rabbit girl: those are in every picture and are what zq_usagi should mean. Afterwards, zq_usagi, riding a bicycle by the sea draws her with all of it, without saying any of it.

Not: zq_usagi, a white rabbit girl with long pink-tipped ears, red eyes and a blue scarf, sitting on a bench. The caption explains her look, so the trigger word learns little, and she only comes out when you spell all of it out again.

The scarf is your choice. Leave it out of every caption and it becomes part of her: she always wears it. Name it (wearing a blue scarf) in the pictures where she has it, and it stays something you can ask for or leave out. Do the same for anything that should stay optional.

Example: a style. Forty paintings with soft watercolour edges and a muted teal-and-orange palette. Trigger word zq_style.

The pictureCaption
A lighthouse at sunsetzq_style, a lighthouse on a cliff at sunset, waves below
A cat on a windowsillzq_style, a cat sleeping on a windowsill, potted plants

Say what is in the picture, never how it is painted: no "watercolour", "soft edges" or "teal". Then zq_style, a busy train station comes out in that style.

One or two plain sentences each, the way you would prompt. HikariLM can write first drafts: attach a picture in Chat and ask what is in it, then cross out the words about what the LoRA should learn and put the trigger word first.

4 Write train.json

Save this as train.json in the folder that holds dataset/. data is read from where train.json is; an absolute path such as /Users/you/dataset works too.

{
  "model": "flux2-klein-base-4b",
  "model_path": "mflux-community/flux-2-klein-base-4b-mflux-q4",
  "data": "dataset/",
  "seed": 42,
  "steps": 50,
  "guidance": 4.0,
  "quantize": null,
  "max_resolution": 1024,
  "low_ram": false,
  "gradient_checkpointing": true,
  "training_loop": { "num_epochs": 20, "batch_size": 1 },
  "optimizer": { "name": "AdamW", "learning_rate": 1e-4 },
  "checkpoint": { "output_path": "training", "save_frequency": 200 },
  "monitoring": {
    "preview_width": 768,
    "preview_height": 768,
    "plot_frequency": 50,
    "generate_image_frequency": 200
  },
  "lora_layers": {
    "targets": [
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_q", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_k", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_v", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_out", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.add_q_proj", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.add_k_proj", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.add_v_proj", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_add_out", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.ff.linear_in", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.ff.linear_out", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.ff_context.linear_in", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "transformer_blocks.{block}.ff_context.linear_out", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 5 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "single_transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_qkv_mlp_proj", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 20 }, "rank": 16 },
      { "module_path": "single_transformer_blocks.{block}.attn.to_out", "blocks": { "start": 0, "end": 20 }, "rank": 16 }
    ]
  }
}

What to change, if anything:

  • num_epochs: how many times it goes through every picture. A step is one picture, so 100 pictures for 20 epochs are 2,000 steps. Fewer pictures, more epochs.
  • save_frequency and generate_image_frequency: every how many steps it saves a checkpoint and draws the previews. Keep them the same, so every checkpoint has previews to judge it by.
  • learning_rate: how big each step is. Lower it (5e-5) if the previews go wrong early.
  • rank: how much the LoRA can hold. 16 is plenty for one style or character; 32 for more, at twice the file size.
  • gradient_checkpointing: on, it needs far less memory and steps take about 30% longer. Turn it off only on a Mac with memory to spare.
  • The lora_layers cover every block of the 4B (5 and 20). Leave them.

5 Check, then train

In Terminal, in the folder with train.json. First a check that reads the config and the pictures without loading the model:

~/.hikarilm/pictures-venv/bin/mflux-train --config train.json --dry-run

When it says Training config validated, train. caffeinate -i keeps the Mac from sleeping while it runs:

caffeinate -i ~/.hikarilm/pictures-venv/bin/mflux-train --config train.json

Keep the Terminal window open; it shows the steps as they go. If it stops (you closed it, the Mac restarted), go on from the last checkpoint instead of starting over:

caffeinate -i ~/.hikarilm/pictures-venv/bin/mflux-train --resume training/checkpoints/0000600_checkpoint.zip

If mflux-train is not found, or does not know flux2-klein-base-4b, update mflux: ~/.hikarilm/pictures-venv/bin/pip install -U mflux.

6 Watch it

Everything goes into training/ (or training_ and the date, if that folder is already there):

  • training/preview/: the preview pictures, by step. This is what to judge by.
  • training/loss/loss.html: the loss chart; open it in the browser. It falls, then flattens.
  • training/checkpoints/: a checkpoint every save_frequency steps.

Early previews look like the plain model. Then the trigger word starts to show. Past the best point, the previews copy your training pictures, lose what the prompt asks for, or turn harsh and oversaturated: that is overtraining. The best checkpoint is often not the last one. Note the step of the previews you like best; you can stop training there with Ctrl+C.

7 Take the LoRA out and try it

A checkpoint zip holds the LoRA as …_adapter.safetensors, beside what the trainer needs to go on. Take it out of the one you picked:

unzip -j training/checkpoints/0001400_checkpoint.zip '*_adapter.safetensors'

and give it a name you will know:

mv 0001400_adapter.safetensors zq-usagi.safetensors

Draw with it to see it work, at full strength (the 1.0 after its name):

~/.hikarilm/pictures-venv/bin/mflux-generate-flux2 --model mflux-community/flux-2-klein-base-4b-mflux-q4 --steps 50 --guidance 4 --seed 1 --prompt "zq_usagi, riding a bicycle along a seaside road, her scarf blowing in the wind, golden hour, wide shot" --lora zq-usagi.safetensors 1.0 --output try.png

Prompting with it. Write the prompt the way you wrote the captions: the trigger word first, then only what this picture should have.

PromptWhat comes out
zq_usagi, riding a bicycle along a seaside road, her scarf blowing in the wind, golden hour, wide shotUsagi, with her white fur, pink-tipped ears and red eyes, though the prompt says none of it, on a bicycle by the sea in evening light.
zq_usagi, reading a book in a library, sitting by the window, rainy day, close-upThe same Usagi, somewhere she never was in your pictures. That is the test that it learned her rather than your pictures.
zq_usagi, wearing a red raincoat instead of her scarf, jumping in puddlesUsagi in a raincoat, if the scarf was named in the captions (step 3). If it never was, it is part of her, and she may keep it on anyway.
a white rabbit girl riding a bicycle by the seaA rabbit girl who may look a little like Usagi, but not reliably: a loaded LoRA leans on every picture somewhat, but it is the trigger word that calls her up.
  • Do not describe her look in the prompt; the trigger word already does, and spelling it out can pull her towards the model's own idea of those words.
  • The trigger word only works with the LoRA loaded (--lora). Without it, zq_usagi is a word the model has never seen and means nothing to it.
  • Too strong (stiff poses, the same face and framing every time, colours burnt) or too weak (she looks off)? Change the 1.0 after the file name: 0.7 for less, 1.2 for more. Strength is chosen when you draw, so the file stays as it is.

8 Draw with it in HikariLM

Upload the .safetensors file to a Hugging Face repo of your own, then add it in Settings, under LoRAs, by its repo name, and pick it under Options when drawing with FLUX.2 klein base 4B. HikariLM puts the trigger word in front of your prompt, so riding a bicycle along a seaside road is enough. Adding the file straight from this Mac comes in HikariLM 6.0. Nothing else is needed from the training folder; keep dataset/ in case you want to train it again.
